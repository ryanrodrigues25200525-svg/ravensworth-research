## Executive summary

Broadcom is the highest-quality AI-infrastructure franchise in semiconductors, and
that is exactly why it is the wrong stock to own at this price. The demand claims
check out: AI semiconductor revenue of $10.8bn in fiscal Q2 2026, up 143% year on
year and *above* guidance; a Q3 guide of $16.0bn, up over 200%; a fiscal-2026 AI
target of $56bn (up ~180%); a fiscal-2027 AI target in excess of $100bn; and a
quarter in which AI bookings reached over $30bn against $10.8bn shipped. The
business is real, the backlog is real, and the six-customer concentration is
genuinely contracted. This is not a claim that AI demand is fake.

The claim is that the multiple has already spent every dollar of it. Broadcom
trades at $392.52 (17 Aug 2026 close) — roughly 45x trailing EBITDA against
SK Hynix at 7.7x, ~20x forward earnings on consensus that already embeds the
fiscal-2027 $100bn+ AI promise, and a 52-week range that peaked at $495 on the
day of the June print. The Street — 45 covering analysts with a Strong-Buy
consensus (score 1.31 on a 1–5 scale, zero Sells, mean target $528) — is not
underwriting the company's numbers; it is underwriting a *further re-rating*
of those numbers toward AI-infrastructure multiples, on top of a stock that
has already re-rated for them.

**Our call is one specific, falsifiable disagreement with the Street.** The
market's inner number for fiscal-2027 AI revenue has drifted to $130–140bn while
management holds its guide at "in excess of $100bn" — and on June 3–4, when the
company reported a record quarter but *held* its guidance, the stock fell 14% in
a day and erased roughly $300bn of market value at the trough. The Street reads
that as a buying opportunity in the best franchise in AI. We read it as the
demonstrated reaction function: this stock does not tolerate "in line." Holding
the guide on September 2 delivers our $340 target; raising it to the whisper
number delivers the Street's. Probability-weighted (30% bear / 45% base / 25%
bull), that disagreement is worth: **Sell, target $340 (~13% below spot).**

## Investment thesis

**The bull case — the structural claims:**

1. **The revenue is arguably the strongest in the sector.** AI semiconductor
   revenue: $8.4bn (Q1 FY26, +106% YoY) → $10.8bn (Q2 FY26, +143% YoY, above
   guide) → $16.0bn guided (Q3 FY26, +200% YoY). Hock Tan on the Q2 call: demand
   for AI semiconductors is "simply insatiable." The fiscal-2026 AI target is
   $56bn, approximately +180% from fiscal 2025, with the second half guided to
   roughly double the first half's ~$19bn.
2. **The revenue is contracted, not spot.** "Multi-year agreements with leading
   industry customers underpin long-term expectations of $100 billion-plus in
   annual AI semiconductor revenue starting in fiscal 2027." Bookings in Q2
   exceeded $30bn against $10.8bn shipped — a book-to-bill near three — and the
   six named core customers include gigawatt-scale commitments (e.g., over
   1 gigawatt of TPU-based compute for Anthropic in 2026).
3. **Custom silicon is the anti-NVDA hedge that keeps winning.** Every
   hyperscaler effort to reduce dependence on merchant GPUs — Google's TPU,
   Meta's MTIA, OpenAI/Anthropic/xAI voyages — routes *more* revenue to
   Broadcom's XPU co-designs, not less. Management expects AI semiconductor
   revenue growth to continue into fiscal 2028 on initiatives with the six core
   customers, and expects "content per gigawatt" to rise as XPU prices increase
   dramatically.
4. **The margin proof is on the record, and operating leverage is real.**
   Q2 FY26: gross margin 77.1%, adjusted EBITDA $15.2bn (69% of revenue, +52%
   YoY), non-GAAP operating margin 67%, and record free cash flow of $10.3bn.
   Semiconductor operating margin of 62% was up 460bp YoY. Consolidated revenue
   grew 48% YoY while EBITDA grew 52% — the machine compounds.

**The bear case — the cyclical and valuation claims:**

5. **The multiple already knows.** At $392.52, Broadcom trades at roughly 45x
   trailing EBITDA (GAAP-style, XBRL operating income plus D&A), 21.3x book
   value, and ~65x trailing GAAP earnings — the number we ourselves flagged in
   the MU initiation as the Street's implicit anchor for re-rating the whole
   complex — versus SK Hynix at 7.7x. On forward earnings, ~20x the
   fiscal-2027 consensus EPS of $19.55 (a non-GAAP number; GAAP TTM EPS is
   $6.00), a multiple that *requires* the $100bn+ AI promise to land on
   schedule. Paying 45x trailing for a company whose own software segment grew
   9% and whose non-AI semiconductors grew 6% is a statement about AI-only
   upside, nothing else.
6. **The reaction function is demonstrated, twice.** June 3–4: record quarter,
   AI revenue above forecast, but guidance *held* rather than raised — and the
   stock fell 14%, with an estimated $300bn of market value destroyed at the
   trough and $1.3tn wiped from global chip stocks in the session. The Street's
   whisper for fiscal-2027 AI has since drifted to $130–140bn. Every dollar of
   that whisper is now priced in. A "hold, not raise" print on September 2 is
   the same trade again.
7. **The second-half ramp is the kind of number that misses.** Fiscal-2026 AI of
   $56bn after a ~$19bn first half implies a second half of ~$37bn — Q3 at
   $16bn guided, Q4 at roughly $21–22bn, a near-60% sequential jump in one
   quarter. That required hockey-stick is being executed while hyperscaler
   capex digestion is the tape's dominant fear — the very names buying the
   silicon (Microsoft −7% YTD, Meta −26% YTD) are being sold for the spending.
   One customer pausing one gigawatt of deployment is all the air this needs.

**The falsifiable question.** The bull case resolves on one date: the September
2 fiscal-Q3 print. If management raises fiscal-2026 AI above $56bn or fiscal-2027
above $100bn — validating the $130–140bn whisper — the Street is right and our
bull case ($433) is the floor, not the ceiling. If it holds the numbers on
another record quarter, the demonstrated June reaction function repeats and our
$340 target is the ceiling. The gap between a held guide and a bid-up whisper is
the entire trade.

## Sector analysis — custom AI silicon

The AI silicon market splits into three lanes: merchant GPU accelerators
(NVIDIA), custom ASIC/XPU co-designs (Broadcom and Marvell), and the
hyperscalers' in-house designs — which, in Google's and Meta's cases, are
themselves Broadcom co-designs. Broadcom's positioning is the strongest of the
three lanes' suppliers: it has no merchant-GPU competitive conflict with its
customers, its Ethernet networking franchise (Tomahawk 6, the industry's first
102.4 Tbps switch, shipping since 2025) is the merchant alternative to NVIDIA's
proprietary NVLink-scale fabrics, and its AI revenue is roughly 60% compute XPUs
and 40% networking — a mix that captures both the accelerator and the
scale-out fabric of every AI cluster, whoever wins the accelerator war.

**What's different this cycle.** AI silicon demand is contracted multi-year and
gigawatt-scale, not spot-bid. Broadcom's six core customers are effectively
pre-paying (bookings ran >2.5x revenue in Q2), and content per gigawatt is
rising as Broadcom's XPU prices escalate with each generation. The unit economics
are stunning: semiconductor operating margin of 62%, adjusted EBITDA margin of
69% at the consolidated level, and a record $10.3bn of quarterly free cash flow.

**What hasn't changed.** The demand base is four to six hyperscalers whose capex
decisions swing ±$20bn in a quarter and whose stocks are being sold *for*
spending. The entire margin structure depends on a second-half doubling that has
not shipped yet. And the valuation frame is unchanged from every prior cycle:
the market pays peak-cycle multiples into peak-growth quarters, then spends
years de-rating. Broadcom's own history — a 15–20x EBITDA multiple for most of
the pre-AI era — is the base rate, not the exception.

**Margins, for context.** Broadcom's 69% adjusted EBITDA margin and 62%
semiconductor operating margin are at the top of the industry — comparable to
the memory complex's extreme prints (Micron 80.4% GAAP operating margin this
fiscal year, per our MU initiation) and above most software franchises. Nobody
disputes the quality of the earnings. The dispute is entirely about what a
multiple of those peak-margin earnings is worth when growth must decelerate
from +200% to +80% to merely continue.

uses the conservative GAAP-style basis, consistent with our MU initiation.

**Capital allocation.** Net debt is $45.3bn (cash $19.6bn against $64.9bn of
long-term debt, May 3, 2026 10-Q) — a modest load for a company generating
record free cash flow of $10.3bn in a quarter. The dividend is token (~$0.60/yr
run-rate versus the share price); the buyback is the return mechanism. The
balance sheet supports whatever incremental AI capacity Broadcom needs; it is
not the issue. The issue is entirely entry valuation versus the rate of
growth deceleration.

demonstration. The demand never weakened — the stock fell anyway purely on
guidance psychology.

## Model

Our model is built from the company's own numbers, not from our own unit
forecasts. Fiscal 2026 revenue: Q1 $19.3bn + Q2 $22.2bn actual, Q3 $29.4bn
guided, Q4 implied by the AI ramp at roughly $35bn (AI ~$21–22bn, non-AI ~$4.5bn,
software ~$9bn) — approximately $106bn, versus a whisper closer to $130–140bn.
Fiscal 2027: AI >$100bn plus non-AI semiconductors ~$20bn and software ~$40bn
(trending at ~10–15% growth) puts the base case near $160bn of revenue.

Next-twelve-months EBITDA (Q3 FY26 through Q2 FY27) is built as: ~$35bn of
remaining FY26 + ~$78bn of the FY27 run-rate, at a 67–69% adjusted EBITDA
margin, arriving at roughly **$97bn of NTM adjusted EBITDA** in our base case.
On the conservative GAAP-style basis used for the comp table (operating income
plus D&A), TTM EBITDA is $41.4bn (54.8% margin) and TTM net income is
depressed by intangible amortization and stock-based comp — another reason we
anchor on EBITDA, not P/E, for a company this acquisition-heavy.

## Positioning &amp; trade mechanics

The mechanics of the short, from live market data (17 Aug 2026 close; options
as of 18 Aug 2026):

- **The short base is thin, not crowded.** 61.2m shares short — **1.31% of
  float**, 2.87 days to cover — and *down* from 70.1m a month earlier: shorts
  covered into the June–August recovery. The squeeze argument against this
  trade is weak by the numbers; there is little fuel to run us over.
- **Ownership is institutional and sticky.** 80.4% institutional, ~1.9%
  insider, on a 4.70bn float. A thin short has no ownership-destabilisation
  channel; this trade works only through multiple re-rating, which is
  exactly what we are claiming.
- **The "cheap" multiple is a non-GAAP construction.** Consensus forward EPS
  of $19.53 (non-GAAP) produces the touted ~20x; GAAP TTM EPS of $6.00 —
  depressed by ~$2bn per quarter of acquisition-intangible amortisation plus
  stock-based comp — puts the trailing P/E near 65x. Anyone underwriting the
  forward multiple is underwriting the exclusions too.
- **Cash return cannot rescue the holder.** TTM GAAP free cash flow is
  $27.2bn (1.5% of market cap); even on the company's own record $10.3bn
  quarterly non-GAAP run-rate, the yield is ~2.2%. The dividend yields
  ~0.15%. The whole return case is multiple expansion on the AI ramp.
- **The options market prices the September 2 print at ±10%.** The ATM
  straddle on the first post-earnings expiry (Sep 4, two sessions after the
  report) is $38.88 — 9.9% of spot — against the *demonstrated* June 3–4
  single-day reaction of −14% when guidance merely held. Our edge is not
  information; it is the gap between a 10%-priced event and a demonstrated
  14% reaction function.
- **Beta 1.47.** This short pays the market's exuberance. In a melt-up it is
  the wrong position — which is risk 5 below.

## Risks — ranked

1. **A guidance raise on September 2.** If management lifts FY26 AI above $56bn
   or FY27 above $100bn, the whisper is validated and the stock re-rates toward
   the Street's $530. This is the primary tail risk and it is binary, dated,
   and imminent — though the thin short base (1.31% of float) limits the
   squeeze fuel if it happens. **Tell:** any upward revision to the $56bn /
   $100bn figures on the Q3 call, or a bookings print materially above the
   $30bn pace.
2. **Custom-silicon share gains accelerate, not just hold.** If hyperscaler
   in-house compute displaces merchant GPUs faster than expected, Broadcom's
   XPU and networking content grows into fiscal 2028 at rates that make ~20x
   forward earnings look cheap. The six-customer concentration is also a
   moat. **Tell:** NVDA data-center growth decelerating while Broadcom AI
   bookings stay above a 2x book-to-bill.
3. **Content-per-gigawatt escalation.** Management explicitly said XPU prices
   "will go up very dramatically." If gigawatt deployments compound faster than
   our revenue build, NTM EBITDA lands at the bull case ($105bn) and the
   de-rating thesis understates the numerator growth. **Tell:** new
   gigawatt-scale customer announcements (Anthropic-class deals) before or on
   the September 2 call.
4. **The software segment re-accelerates.** VMware/Infrastructure Software grew
   9% in Q2 — if the ANNEXUS cycle re-accelerates software to mid-teens
   growth, the "two-engine" bull argument gets real traction. **Tell:** software
   revenue growth above 12–15% in the Q3 print, or a large private-cloud
   renewal announcement.
5. **A rising tide lifts all multiples.** The S&P 500 is at records, the 10-year
   Treasury is approaching 4.75%, and the market has repeatedly bought
   AI-infrastructure dips. In a melt-up, 45x-EBITDA names are the first things
   that double — and at beta 1.47 this short pays for the market's exuberance
   before it pays for the company's. The short base is thin (1.31% of float),
   so our cover is thin too. **Tell:** continued broad tech leadership into
   and through the September 2 print, especially on rate relief.
6. **We are short a contracted backlog.** The $30bn quarterly bookings number
   is a genuine de-risking of the ramp. If bookings convert to revenue on
   schedule *and* hyperscalers raise capex again (Microsoft, Meta both report
   this week and next), the dated, binary nature of our thesis works against
   us on timing. **Tell:** hyperscaler capex language shifting from
   "digesting" back to "accelerating" before September 2.
